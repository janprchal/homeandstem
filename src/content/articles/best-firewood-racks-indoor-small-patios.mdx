## How to Choose a Firewood Rack

### Decide indoor or outdoor first

Indoor racks are small and decorative, and they hold a few days of wood. Outdoor racks are larger and sit on a patio, deck, or beside the house, where the stack can season in the air. Most people end up with both: a big stack outside and a small rack by the fire.

### Match the size to how you burn

A rack that holds a few days of logs covers casual fireplace use. If you heat with a wood stove, look at the larger outdoor sizes. The quarter cord and eighth of a face cord figures in the listings are the manufacturers' own numbers, based on standard 24-inch logs.

### Keep the stack off the ground

Wood that sits on soil or a damp deck soaks up moisture and rots. A rack lifts the logs so air moves underneath. Racks with a raised base or legs do this best.

### Think about the indoor stack

Bring in only a few days of wood at a time. Wood stored outside can carry insects, and a small indoor rack keeps the amount manageable. Check your stove or fireplace manual for clearance rules before you place a rack nearby.
