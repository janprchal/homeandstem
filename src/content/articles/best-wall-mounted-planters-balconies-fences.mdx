## What to Look for in a Wall-Mounted or Rail Planter

### How your railing or fence is actually shaped

A flat-topped rail 3.5 to 5.5 inches wide fits the straddle-style boxes from Root & Vessel and HC Companies. A round rail, an angled cap, or a picket fence needs a hook-and-bracket style like the Tosnail buckets or the LaLaGreen boxes instead. Measure before you buy, since a box built for a flat rail simply won't sit level on a round one.

### Weight when it's fully planted and watered

Wet potting soil is heavy, and a planter hanging off the outside of a rail puts that weight on a bracket or a molded lip, not the ground. Check the fence or railing you're mounting to for that kind of load, especially with a metal box like the LaLaGreen that holds more soil than the plastic options.

### Drainage and what's underneath

A planter draining onto a lower balcony, a walkway, or a neighbor's patio is a real problem, not a minor one. Look for a planter with a drip tray or a sealable drainage plug like the HC Companies box, or plan to set a tray underneath anything without one.

### Material for your actual climate

Plastic resists cracking better in freeze-thaw climates but can fade with years of direct sun. Metal holds its shape and color longer but heats up faster in full sun, which matters for what you plant in it. Either works fine for most of a growing season; it's the years of exposure that separate them.
