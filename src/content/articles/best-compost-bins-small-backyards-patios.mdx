## What to Look for in a Compost Bin

### Size versus how much waste you actually generate

A 246-gallon bin sounds efficient until it sits half-empty for a year. Match capacity to what your household realistically produces: a couple with a small garden usually does fine with 40 to 65 gallons, while anyone composting a lot of yard trimmings benefits from an expandable option like the Geobin.

### Turning method

Tumblers speed up decomposition because turning mixes in oxygen, but they take more effort to assemble and, once full, more effort to rotate. A stationary bin or an in-ground unit asks less of you day to day but takes longer to finish a batch. Neither is wrong, it depends on whether you want fast compost or low-maintenance compost.

### Pest resistance

Rodents and flies are drawn to food scraps more than yard waste. A fully enclosed bin with a secure lid, or a worm bin kept properly balanced, keeps pests out far better than an open wire cage. If you're only composting leaves and grass clippings, an open design is less of a concern.

### Material and durability

Thin plastic panels can crack after a couple of winters outdoors, especially in bins that get moved or knocked around. Look for UV-resistant plastic or a galvanized metal frame if the bin will sit outside year-round rather than get stored away each season.
