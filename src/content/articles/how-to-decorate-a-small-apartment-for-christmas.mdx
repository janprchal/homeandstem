A small apartment has no floor space to spare, and many leases ban nails in the walls. Both limits shape the plan. Put the tree where it is safe, hang lights on the walls and windows with clips that come off cleanly, and let a timer turn everything off at bedtime.

This takes about **2 hours** and costs **under $50** for the three essentials below, if you already own a tree and ornaments. If you still need a tree, see our guide to [artificial trees for small spaces](/best-artificial-christmas-trees-small-spaces).

<StepBlock number={1} title="Measure and place the tree">
Walk the room before you unbox anything. The National Fire Protection Association says a tree should stand at least 3 feet from any heat source, including radiators, fireplaces, space heaters, candles, and heating vents. Keep it clear of doors and exits too. In a small apartment, that rules out the spot next to the radiator, so look for a corner or a stretch of wall by a window. If you buy an artificial tree, look for a label that says it is fire resistant or fire retardant.

<CalloutBox variant="tip" title="Metal trees and electric lights">
Don't put electric lights or electric ornaments on an artificial tree with metallic leaves or branch coverings. The Electrical Safety Foundation says to skip them on those trees.
</CalloutBox>
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={2} title="Hang lights with wall-safe clips">
Command's clear indoor light clips hold string lights to painted walls, finished wood, glass, tile, metal, and laminate. 3M says to use one clip for every 2 feet of lights, so a 33-foot strand needs about 17 clips. Clean the spot with rubbing alcohol first, and keep the room between 50 and 105°F while you apply them. If you just painted, wait seven days. 3M also says not to hang the clips over beds or on wallpaper.

The clips are meant for lightweight strands. Check the weight limit on the package before you hang anything heavier, like a full garland.
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={3} title="Check your lights before you plug them in">
Inspect each strand while it is unplugged. The Consumer Product Safety Commission says to look for frayed insulation, loose connections, and exposed wire, and to throw out any set that shows them. Use lights marked for indoor use, and look for the label of an independent testing lab such as UL or ETL. Read the box for how many strands you can connect end to end. The CPSC also warns against stringing together more than three sets of incandescent lights, and LED sets have their own limit, so follow the label.

<CalloutBox variant="tip" title="Check the lab label on the box">
The listing for the JMEXSUSS lights below says UL Certified. The label on the box is the final word, so look for it before you plug the lights in.
</CalloutBox>
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={4} title="Plug the lights into a timer">
The NFPA says to turn off tree lights before you leave home or go to bed. A timer does it for you. The GE timer below uses pushpins in 30-minute steps, so you can set the lights to come on at dusk and go off at bedtime. It has an override switch for nights you want them on longer. Each timer has two outlets, so one timer can run two strands. Don't overload the outlet. The Electrical Safety Foundation warns that too many decorations on one outlet can overheat and cause a fire.

Tape cords along the wall edge instead of running them under a rug, and keep them out of walking paths.
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={5} title="Go vertical and keep decorations flame-resistant">
Use the walls and windows first, since they cost you no floor space. Frame a window with a lit strand, or run lights along the top of a bookcase. Set small greenery and ornaments on shelves and window ledges instead of the floor. Choose trimmings that are flame resistant or flame retardant, as the NFPA advises, and leave candles off the tree. The NFPA says more than one-third of home decoration fires start with candles, so battery-powered flameless candles are a better fit near greenery. When January comes, our [holiday storage guide](/best-storage-solutions-holiday-decorations) shows how to pack everything into a small closet.
</StepBlock>

## Tips and Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Don't put the tree near a heat vent or radiator. The 3-foot rule applies even in a small room.
- Don't hang clips on wallpaper or over a bed.
- Pull the clip tab slowly and straight down when you take lights off. Never pull it toward you. Stretch it at least 6 inches until the strip lets go, or you can damage the wall.
- Don't leave lights on when you sleep or leave the apartment.
- Don't swap indoor and outdoor lights. Some sets are made for indoor use only, and others for outdoor use only.
- Don't plug every strand into one outlet. Overloaded outlets can overheat.
