Poinsettias are tougher than they look, but three ordinary things at home wear them down: a foil sleeve that traps water, a cold draft, and a watering can used on a calendar instead of on the soil. Colorado State Extension says a well-cared-for poinsettia can keep its color until mid-March.

This routine takes about **30 minutes** to set up and costs **under $50** if you buy every item below. After that you spend a few minutes a week on it. The last step covers the spring cutback if you want to keep the plant for next year. Repotting comes later, in late spring, and our [repotting guide](/how-to-repot-a-houseplant) explains how.

<WhatYouNeedList variant="full" items={frontmatter.essentials} />

<StepBlock number={1} title="Unwrap the pot and set up drainage">
Poinsettias come in a decorative foil or plastic sleeve. The sleeve holds water around the roots, and constant wetness rots them. Take the plant out of the sleeve before you water, or punch a few holes in the bottom so water can escape. Then set the pot on a clear saucer. You can see when the saucer fills, and the water has somewhere to go.

<CalloutBox variant="tip" title="Bringing one home in cold weather">
Temperatures below 50°F can damage a poinsettia. If it is colder than that outside, wrap the plant before you carry it from the store to your car.
</CalloutBox>
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={2} title="Pick a bright spot away from drafts">
Give the plant bright, indirect light. Six hours a day is ideal. Keep the leaves off cold window glass, because outdoor cold passes through the pane and damages them. Aim for a room temperature of 65 to 70°F. Move the pot away from exterior doors, heating vents, radiators, fireplaces, and space heaters. Kansas State Extension says a spot near an outside door is as bad as a spot near an air vent.
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={3} title="Water when the soil is dry, then drain the pot">
Check the soil surface every day or two. Water when it feels dry to a light touch, or when the pot feels light in your hand. Don't let the plant dry out far enough to wilt. When you water, carry the pot to the sink, soak the soil, and let the water run out of the bottom. Empty the saucer afterward. NC State Extension names overwatering as a common reason poinsettias decline after the holidays.

<CalloutBox variant="tip" title="Use a meter as a second opinion">
The touch test and the lift test work once you know your plant. A moisture meter helps if you are unsure. Push the probe in near the roots, read the dial from dry to wet, and water when it reads dry. Wipe the probe between plants.
</CalloutBox>
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={4} title="Hold off on fertilizer until you see new growth">
A poinsettia needs no food while it is in color. Start feeding when new green leaves or stems appear, which happens in late winter or early spring. An all-purpose plant food works. For indoor plants, Miracle-Gro's label says to mix 1/2 teaspoon per gallon of water and reapply every 2 weeks. You mix it in the watering can, so feeding and watering happen in one trip.
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={5} title="Cut it back when the bracts fade">
The colorful parts of a poinsettia are bracts, which are modified leaves. When they fade and drop in late winter or early spring, you can cut the plant back. Extension guides give different heights, from about 4 to 8 inches, so cut to about 6 inches. The plant will look bare for a while, then push out new growth. Poinsettia sap makes tools sticky, so wash your snips after use.
</StepBlock>

## Tips and Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Remove the foil sleeve or punch drainage holes before you water.
- Keep the leaves off cold window glass, even for one night.
- Water by checking the soil. Pot size, room temperature, and soil mix all change how fast the plant dries.
- Skip fertilizer while the bracts are colored. Wait for new growth.
- Pruning or pinching after September 1 can delay flowering, according to Mississippi State Extension.
- Don't count on a rebloom. Leonard Perry of the University of Vermont says there is no guarantee your poinsettia will bloom again next December, even with year-round care.
