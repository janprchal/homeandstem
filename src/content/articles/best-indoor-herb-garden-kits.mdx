Fresh basil from the supermarket wilts in three days. A kit on your counter keeps producing for months, and a handful of leaves costs pennies once the plants are growing. The six picks below cover the two main routes: hydroponic gardens with a built-in light, and simple soil setups for a sunny windowsill.

Hydroponic kits cost more, but they grow faster and don't depend on window light. Soil kits cost under $25 and suit a bright south-facing window. If you want to grow outdoors or on a balcony instead, start with our guide to a [container herb garden](/how-to-start-container-herb-garden-balcony). ## What to Look for in an Indoor Herb Garden Kit

### Hydroponic or soil

Hydroponic kits grow herbs in water with nutrients, under a built-in light. They cost $55 to $120, grow quickly, and work in a window-less corner. Soil setups cost under $25 and feel more familiar, but they rely on window light and a watering routine. If your kitchen has a bright window, soil is a fine place to start.

### Number of pods

Count what you cook with. Four to six herbs cover most kitchens, so ten pods already leaves room to experiment. Sixteen pods suit a household that eats a lot of fresh greens.

### Light quality and timers

Look for full-spectrum LEDs and a built-in timer. The timer matters more than it sounds, since plants need a steady day and night cycle. Adjustable height helps as basil and mint grow taller.

### Counter space and outlets

Hydroponic gardens need a flat spot and an outlet. Measure the space under your cabinets, because taller plants will need clearance. Check the water reservoir size too: a bigger tank means fewer refills.

### Pods and refills

Pods and nutrient liquid are a recurring cost. Many kits accept generic pods, so you aren't tied to one brand's seeds. Check this before you buy.
