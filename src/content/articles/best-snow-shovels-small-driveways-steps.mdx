## What to Look for in a Snow Shovel

### Scoop, pusher or combo

A scoop lifts and throws snow, which suits steps, decks and narrow paths. A pusher slides snow forward, which suits flat pavement and light, fresh snow. A combo blade tries to do both. For a small driveway plus steps, most households do best with one scoop and one pusher, or a single combo shovel.

### Blade size and material

Wider blades move more snow per pass but weigh more when loaded. Plastic blades are light and gentle on decks. Metal blades hold up better on packed snow and ice. NBC Select suggests keeping both a plastic and a metal shovel on hand if your weather varies.

### Handle and weight

A D-grip is easier to hold in gloves than a straight handle. Dual-handle designs shift the lifting to your lower hand, and light shovels tire you less over a long job. One ergonomist has noted that no single shovel is the right fit for everyone, so choose by how it feels in your hands.

### Snow type

Shovels work best on light snow and small areas. If you get heavy, wet snow regularly, you will want a sturdier blade and a smaller scoop so you do not overload it.

### Price

You do not need to spend $70 to clear a short driveway. A $30 shovel in the right style beats a $75 one in the wrong style. Check the same model at Home Depot or Lowe's before you buy, since Amazon is not always cheapest.
