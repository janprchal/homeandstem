Most houseplants don't die in winter from the cold. They die from three quiet changes: less light, dry heated air, and a watering habit that stayed the same while the plant stopped growing. Fix those three and a plant that looked miserable in January will push out new leaves by March.

This routine takes about **1 hour** to set up and costs **under $50** for the essentials below. Most of it is moving pots and changing how often you reach for the watering can. If you plan to repot anything, do it in spring instead. Our [repotting guide](/how-to-repot-a-houseplant) covers the timing.

<StepBlock number={1} title="Move plants closer to the light">
From November to February the sun sits lower and the days run short, so a spot that was bright in July can turn dim. Move your light-hungry plants to the brightest window you have, ideally south or west facing. Clean the glass while you're there, because winter grime blocks more light than you'd expect. Rotate each pot a quarter turn every week or two so the plant doesn't lean toward the glass.

<CalloutBox variant="tip" title="Keep leaves off the cold glass">
Leaves pressed against a freezing window can get frost damage overnight. Leave a few inches of gap, or slide a sheer curtain between the plant and the pane on the coldest nights.
</CalloutBox>
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={2} title="Move plants away from drafts and heat vents">
Plants dislike sudden swings more than steady cool air. Pull pots away from exterior doors, leaky windows, radiators, and heating vents. A vent that blows warm, dry air across a fern will crisp the leaf tips within days. Aim for a room that stays between roughly 60 and 75°F, which suits most common houseplants.
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={3} title="Water less, and check the soil first">
Growth slows in winter, so roots drink less. The classic mistake is keeping the summer schedule and drowning a plant that isn't using the water. Push a finger two inches into the soil, or use a moisture meter to check at root depth. Water only when the soil is dry at that depth, then let the excess drain out of the pot. Many plants need water every two to three weeks instead of every week.

<CalloutBox variant="tip" title="Why a meter helps">
A battery-free moisture meter costs about $10 and removes the guessing. Push the probe in, read the dial from dry to wet, and pull it out. Wipe the probe between pots so you don't carry salts or pests from one plant to the next.
</CalloutBox>
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={4} title="Raise the humidity around your plants">
Heated indoor air often drops below 30 percent humidity, while tropical plants like calatheas, ferns, and monstera prefer 40 to 60 percent. Brown, crispy leaf edges are the usual sign. A small cool mist humidifier near your plant grouping is the most reliable fix. Cluster the pots together as well, since plants raise the humidity around each other as they release moisture. Misting only helps for a few minutes, so skip it.
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={5} title="Add a grow light on the dimmest days">
If your brightest window still only gets a few hours of weak sun, a grow light fills the gap. Clip or mount a full-spectrum LED strip a few inches above the leaves and put it on a timer for 10 to 12 hours a day. A plug-in timer means you set it once and forget it. See our [grow light buying guide](/best-grow-lights-buying-guide) for other options if you have a large collection.
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={6} title="Pause fertilizer, dust the leaves, and check for pests">
Most plants rest in winter, and fertilizer on a resting plant builds up as salt in the soil. Stop feeding from late fall until you see new growth in spring. Wipe dust from broad leaves with a damp cloth so they can catch every bit of available light. Dry air also invites spider mites and fungus gnats, so look under leaves and at the soil surface every time you water.
</StepBlock>

## Tips and Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Don't repot or prune hard in winter. The plant has little energy to recover, so save the big jobs for spring.
- Use room-temperature water. Ice-cold tap water straight from the faucet can shock roots.
- Don't place a humidifier so close that the mist wets the leaves or the wall behind it. Leave a couple of feet of space and wipe the tank regularly.
- Treat succulents and cacti differently. Most need almost no water in winter, so check them far less often than leafy tropicals.
- Don't panic over a few yellow lower leaves. Some leaf drop is normal as light drops. Widespread yellowing with wet soil points to overwatering.
- Recheck your setup in January. Light and heating patterns shift, so a pot that did well in November may need a new spot.
