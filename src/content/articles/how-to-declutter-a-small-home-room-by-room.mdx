A small home fills up fast because every item has to live somewhere close. When the closet is full, things pile up on the counter, the chair, and the floor. The fix is to own less, then give what remains a home.

This plan takes about **four weekends**, one for each part of the home, and costs **under $65** if you buy every item below. Work in short sessions. Oklahoma State University Extension suggests a timer set for 15 or 30 minutes, and University of Georgia Extension suggests 10 to 15 minutes a day.

<StepBlock number={1} title="Set up four boxes and a rule for each room">
Oklahoma State University Extension recommends the four-box method: label boxes keep, donate, sell, and trash, and sort every item into one. University of Georgia Extension adds a fifth category, recycle, and a "maybe" pile for items you cannot decide on. Pick the system you will use. Put the labels on the boxes before you pick up the first item.

Work on one small area at a time, such as a drawer, a shelf, or a closet. Finish it before you move on. Finishing a small area gives you momentum, according to UGA.
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={2} title="Start with the kitchen">
The kitchen is where food, tools, and paper pile up. Clear one cabinet or drawer at a time. Toss expired food and duplicate tools. Then organize what is left. Our guides cover the three hardest spots: the [small pantry](/how-to-organize-a-small-pantry), the [fridge](/best-fridge-organizers), and the cabinet [under the sink](/best-under-sink-organizers-kitchens-bathrooms).

<CalloutBox variant="note" title="Do the pantry and fridge as a pair">
Both hold food that expires, so you will throw out the same kind of items. Do them in one session and your trash bag will be full for once.
</CalloutBox>
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={3} title="Move to the bathroom and clear the medicine cabinet">
Bathrooms have the fewest big items and the most small ones: half-empty bottles, expired creams, and old medicine. The FDA says the best way to dispose of most unused or expired medicines, prescription and over the counter, is a drug take-back program. Many pharmacies have drop-off boxes or mail-back envelopes. Do not flush a medicine unless it is on the FDA flush list or the label says to. If no take-back option is available, the FDA says almost all other medicines can go in the household trash with the precautions on its website.

Keep cleaning supplies and medicine out of reach of children, in a locked cabinet.
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={4} title="Do the bedroom and closet">
Take every item out of one drawer or one section of the closet. UGA suggests asking four questions of each item: when did I last use it, do I love it, how hard would it be to replace, and would a photo keep the memory? Keep what you wear. Put the rest in the donate box. Fold the keepers and store them in bins you can label.

<CalloutBox variant="tip" title="Skip the sentimental items at first">
UGA advises starting with easy items, such as duplicates and things you never use, and saving sentimental objects for last. Oklahoma State adds that you are not responsible for someone else's memories.
</CalloutBox>
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={5} title="Finish with the living room and entry">
These are the rooms where everything lands: mail, shoes, bags, and toys. Clear every flat surface first. Items that belong in another room go in a bin to carry there, so you do not wander off in the middle of a session. Then sort the shelves and the entry closet with the same four boxes.
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={6} title="Get the boxes out of the house this week">
UGA says to remove donated and discarded items fast so you do not second-guess the decision. Oklahoma State says to take items to a donation center instead of leaving them in your car for weeks. Ask for a receipt if you plan to claim the donation on your taxes. For items you want to sell, list them on a local sales site, and set a date to donate whatever has not sold.
</StepBlock>

<StepBlock number={7} title="Keep it up with one-in, one-out">
Oklahoma State recommends a one-in, one-out rule: when a new item comes into the house, a similar one goes out. Put things away every day, and clear out new piles once a week. A 10 minute reset after dinner is enough for a small home.
</StepBlock>

## Tips and Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Do not start a second room before you finish the first one.
- Do not buy bins before you sort. UGA says to assign each category a physical space and keep only what fits.
- Do not keep a "maybe" pile forever. Set a date, and if you have not missed the items by then, donate them.
- Do not do it all in one day. Short sessions are easier to repeat.
- Do not skip the car trip to the donation center.
