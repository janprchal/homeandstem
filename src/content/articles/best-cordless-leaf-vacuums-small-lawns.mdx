Search "cordless leaf vacuum" on Amazon and most results that show up are actually corded, including the popular WORX Trivac line. A true battery-powered blower/vacuum combo is a smaller category than the search results suggest. Getting the distinction right matters too, since a small lawn doesn't need the extra weight or price of a professional-grade unit built for acreage. Below are the two real cordless options worth considering, and the specs that actually separate them.

If you already own a [cordless leaf blower](/ego-lb5302-vs-greenworks-40v-leaf-blower), check whether its battery matches one of the picks below. Sharing a battery platform between tools is the easiest way to avoid buying (and charging) yet another proprietary pack.

## What to Look for in a Cordless Leaf Vacuum

### Vacuum mode, not just blow mode

Plenty of listings labeled "leaf vacuum" are blowers with an optional vacuum attachment sold separately, or not sold at all. Check the product description for an included vacuum tube and collection bag, not just the word "vacuum" in the title.

### Battery platform

A leaf vacuum sold as "tool only" needs a battery and charger from the same voltage line, and buying those separately can add $50 to $100 to the real price. If you already own cordless yard tools, matching that platform turns a second tool into a much cheaper purchase.

### CFM and mulch ratio

CFM (cubic feet per minute) measures how much air moves through the vacuum, and a higher number clears wet or matted leaves faster. The mulch ratio tells you how much a full bag of leaves shrinks down, useful for anyone who has to carry the bag to a curb or a compost bin afterward.

### Runtime for a small lawn

Cordless tools trade raw power for battery life, and a small lawn rarely needs more than 20 to 30 minutes of continuous runtime per session. Don't pay extra for a battery built for a two-acre property if a quarter-acre lot is all you're clearing.
