## What to Look for in a Fall Porch Planter

### Size relative to your porch

A planter that looks right in a product photo can look undersized once it's sitting next
to a full-height front door. As a rule of thumb, a planter under 14" tall gets lost next to
a standard door. Anything 18" and up reads as intentional from the street.

### Material and weather resistance

Resin and magnesium-oxide composites handle freeze-thaw cycles better than painted ceramic,
which can crack once temperatures drop. If you're in a region with real winter, a
composite or plastic planter you can bring in or empty out is the safer long-term buy.

### Drainage

Every planter on this list has a drainage hole, but check before you buy elsewhere. Fall
rain sitting in a planter with no drainage is one of the fastest ways to rot mum roots
before the season ends.

<CalloutBox variant="tip" title="Styling tip">
  Group planters in odd numbers. One large urn plus two smaller flanking pots reads more
  intentional than a single matched pair on its own.
</CalloutBox>
