All three products below come in jugs sized for a few steps, a porch and a short walkway. We compared what each one is made of, how cold it works, and what you pay per pound. ## How to Choose a Pet-Safe Ice Melt

### Check the ingredient list

"Pet-safe" is a marketing term, and no standard defines it. The three products here fall into two groups. Safe Paw and Morton are urea-based with added glycols and contain no salt or chloride. Natural Rapport is an acetate blend that still contains some chloride salts. Even the gentler formulas can upset a pet's stomach if it eats enough of them, so keep every jug out of reach.

### Match the temperature to your winters

Both urea-based picks are rated down to 10°F. That works in most of the country but falls short in a deep freeze. If your nights fall below 10°F often, magnesium chloride melts work at lower temperatures, but they are chloride-based, and vets say they can still irritate paws and cause harm if eaten.

### Compare price per pound

An 8 lb jug at $26.96 and a 10 lb jug at $25.16 look close, but the second costs about $2.52 per pound against $3.37. Check the weight first, on multipacks in particular, then divide.

### If you have a cat

Vetstreet says urea-based ice melt should be avoided around cats, and it warns that propylene glycol can damage a cat's red blood cells. Safe Paw and Morton both use urea and glycols, so both fall under that caution. If a cat goes outside where you spread ice melt, ask your vet which type to use.

### Use a thin layer and wipe paws

A thin, even layer works better than a thick one, and too much leaves a slushy mess that sticks to paws. The ASPCA recommends wiping or rinsing your dog's paws after a walk. Store each jug sealed and out of reach.
